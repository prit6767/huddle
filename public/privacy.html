◍ Huddle — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 July 2026

Huddle is an AI assistant you add to a group chat (Slack, Google Chat, and others). You address it, it answers — searching the web when the answer depends on current information. This policy explains, in plain terms, what it does with your data. It is written to reflect how the software actually behaves.

What Huddle processes

  • Messages you address to it. When you @mention Huddle or message it directly, the text of that message — and a short window of recent channel messages, for context — is sent to Anthropic (the maker of Claude) to generate an answer, and may be used to perform web searches. Huddle only answers messages that are addressed to it.
  • Short-term context. To follow a conversation, Huddle keeps a small, bounded buffer of the most recent messages (about 20) in a channel where it is used, stored in Cloudflare D1. It is used only to give your questions context and is continuously overwritten as new messages arrive.
  • Planning data. If you use the planning feature, the preferences and options for that plan are stored so your group can view and vote on them.
  • Workspace connection. For each workspace that installs Huddle, an access token is stored so the bot can post replies. It is kept separately from any content and is never exposed publicly.

Who your data is shared with

  • Anthropic — message text is sent to Anthropic's API to generate answers and power web search. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Cloudflare — Huddle runs on Cloudflare Workers and stores its limited data in Cloudflare D1.
  • No one else. Huddle does not sell your data, share it with advertisers, or use it to build profiles of you.

What Huddle does not do

  • It does not process messages you don't address to it, beyond the short context buffer.
  • It does not retain your messages long-term or use them to train models.
  • It invents nothing — no ratings, reviews, or claims it can't attribute to a source.

Retention & deletion

The context buffer is small and continuously overwritten, so old messages age out on their own. Removing Huddle from your workspace stops all processing for that workspace. To request deletion of any stored data, contact us at the address below.

Contact

Questions or data requests: see the support page. Huddle is open source — the code is public, so you can verify exactly what it does.
